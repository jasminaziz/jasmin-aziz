# Site Stranger Audit — jasminaziz.co.uk
**Date:** 2026-06-15
**Agent:** site-stranger (Claude Sonnet 4.6)
**URL audited:** https://jasminaziz.co.uk
**Pages audited:** / , /services, /about, /contact, /legal
**Previous stranger report:** None
**Entry context:** Visitor arriving from LinkedIn; decision-maker at a charity or purpose-led organisation. Knows Jasmin works in some "fractional" capacity. Asking: what does fractional mean in practice, and is this person right for us?

---

## Verdict

A skeptical stranger arriving from LinkedIn would stay through the homepage and probably click through to Services. They would not bounce in the first five seconds. They might leave Services without contacting because there is no price anchor and the "right for" sections describe situations rather than outcomes. The journey from landing to the enquiry form is clear; the journey from the enquiry form to confidence is not fully closed. The fractional question — the most important question for this specific visitor — is answered well on the site, but it is answered too far down the page to catch the visitor who does not scroll all the way.

---

## Check 1: The homepage five-second test

Above the fold: cobalt nav bar (wordmark "Jasmin Aziz", three links), then the H1 "Strategic marketing & communications for the work that matters." Below that, the hero lead in italic periwinkle: "Fractional or per project. AI fluency built into how I deliver." Then a cobalt pill: "See how I work →".

**Can a stranger tell what this is or does?**
Yes. "Strategic marketing & communications" is unambiguous.

**Can they tell who it is for?**
Partially. "For the work that matters" is a soft signal toward purpose-led work. The stranger arriving from LinkedIn with context will recognise themselves. A cold visitor without that context will not be certain until they scroll. The phrase "values-led organisations" first appears in the diagnosis band, which is below the fold.

**Is there one clear next step without scrolling?**
Yes. "See how I work →" is the only CTA in the hero and it is correctly placed.

**Finding: moderate.** The hero qualifies the audience below the fold. A decision-maker at a charity who arrives without LinkedIn context has to scroll before they know this is for them. "For the work that matters" is doing the work of "for values-led organisations" and it is not strong enough to close the gap. The hero lead currently reads "Fractional or per project." That line names the format; it has space to also name the audience. No fix required to homepage layout — the question is whether the lead line can carry both jobs with a small addition.

## Check 2: The stranger's walk

### Homepage

**Job:** Establish who Jasmin is, what she does, and open the door to inquiry.
**Visitor question:** Is this person relevant to my organisation?
**Next step at bottom:** "Get in touch →" pill, with a secondary line offering Services and About as alternatives. Clean decision point; the page does not trail off.

The "How I work" section on the homepage (fourth content section, an ochre-tinted band) contains the clearest one-sentence definition of fractional on the page: "Fractional means holding the marketing and communications function from inside the team for a defined period, not freelance support delivered from outside it." This is the answer the visitor from the brief needs. It is buried as the fourth section on a long page. A visitor who scrolls 60% and stops misses it entirely.

### Services

**Job:** Explain what Jasmin sells, how engagements run, and give a path to contact.
**Visitor question:** What would working with her actually look like, and is it right for us?
**Next step at bottom:** "Get in touch →" pill in a sticky aside, plus email and About link. Clean.

The anchor bar is useful. Service descriptions are unusually specific and honest ("Not a theoretical overview, not a general introduction to AI."). The "Right for" blocks describe situations rather than company sizes — correct for an enquiry-based service.

**Finding: high, specific to the fractional question.** The best fractional definition on the site is in the Services tier 2 "Fractional" shape block: "I hold the marketing and communications function from inside the team for a defined period. Not freelance support delivered from outside: fractional is strategic ownership." A charity director reading this who has never hired fractional before is making a more unfamiliar decision than one buying a brand project. They need to reach this definition quickly. It sits below five service definitions — approximately 1,800 words into a long page. This is the single highest-cost placement decision on the site for the specific visitor described in the brief.

**Finding: moderate.** The fractional "shape" section has no "Right for" component in the ochre-bordered format used for the five services. The qualifying copy ("Suits organisations without a marketing and communications lead, those in a growth or change phase, or teams strong on delivery but needing strategic leadership above them") is embedded in the body paragraph. Separating it into a "Right for" block would make it scannable without requiring the visitor to read the full description first.

### About

**Job:** Give the stranger enough personal and professional context to decide to trust Jasmin.
**Visitor question:** Has she done this before with organisations like mine?
**Next step at bottom:** "Tell me what you're working on." pill with secondary links to Edit AI and Substack. Clean.

The background section earns its length. The museums origin is specific and grounds the "interpretation" framing that runs through the rest of the copy. The eight-year sector summary is concrete. The AI fluency paragraph names a real thing she built and operates. The "Work in practice" section is the strongest outcome writing on the site.

**Finding: moderate.** The About page carries no named clients, sectors, or example outcomes. The credentials are biographical rather than evidential. The brief states the audience is "charity and purpose-led organisations mid-vetting" — these organisations compare consultants, and comparators often list named clients or case references. The writing is strong enough to hold the page without them, but a single concrete anchor (a named sector, a situation type, a result type) would close the gap between "interesting background" and "I can see this working for us."

### Contact

**Job:** Remove friction between intent and message sent.
**Visitor question:** What do I need to tell her to get things started?
**Next step at bottom:** Social and external links in footer. Fine; by this point the form has been submitted.

The contact page is well-built. "You don't need to have everything figured out before reaching out" removes a common blocker. The "I'm not sure yet" checkbox option handles an uncertain visitor. "Tell me a bit more (optional)" removes the pressure to write a brief. The two-working-day response commitment is credible and specific. The success message confirms the loop is closed.

**Minor finding:** The sentence "A basic understanding of organizational challenges is sufficient to begin a conversation" (rendered in the intro below the heading) is more formal and slightly clinical than the rest of the page copy. It reads differently to the warmth of "Tell me what you're working on." and is also the only place on the site that uses American spelling ("organizational" rather than "organisational"). Check the live source.

### Legal

Informational only. No job in conversion terms. No dead end: it links back via footer. Set aside.

## Check 3: The money page

Services is the money page.

**Is the offer clear in one reading?** Yes, broadly. The two-tier structure (services then engagement shapes) is logical and the anchor bar makes it scannable.

**Is it obvious what is included?** Yes at the service level. The communications audit description specifies scope precisely: "Audiences, channels, content output across the team, brand consistency, capacity, and whether the strategy underneath the activity is doing real work." Deliverable: "a written assessment and prioritised recommendations, ready to take to a board, a leadership team, or straight into a plan." That is board-ready language and it is the right call for this audience.

**Is it obvious what happens next?** Yes. Each service has its own CTA pill.

**Outcome ledger — Services copy:**

Items that name what changes for the buyer (outcomes):
- Audit: "finds the gap between what is being produced and what the organisation actually needs"
- Brand: "what the organisation stands for, who it's for, how it talks about itself, and what makes it distinct"
- Content: "teams produce content reactively because the system for doing it any other way isn't there. This is that system."
- Fractional: "strategic ownership" (framing, not a result)

Items that name what gets done but not what changes (deliverables only):
- Campaign: "the audiences, the positioning, the channel approach, the messaging, and the phased plan your team executes against" — the plan is the deliverable; the result (the campaign lands, the audience acts, the fundraising target moves) is not named.
- AI training: "tools worth using, prompting that produces consistent output, brand voice setup, safe practice" — what the team gets out of the training (produces consistent AI-assisted content independently) is implied, not stated.

**Finding: moderate.** Campaign and AI training copy leans on deliverables. The question these sections must answer is: what does the organisation's communications capability look like after this engagement that it did not look like before? Jasmin cannot guarantee specific results, but she can name the state: a team with a strategy to execute against, or a team that produces AI-assisted content without external input at every step. The "Right for" blocks partially compensate by describing the incoming situation, but the outgoing situation is left to the visitor to imagine.

**No price information** appears anywhere. One mention: "Pricing is scoped per project and arrives at proposal stage." Defensible for an enquiry-based consultancy with variable scope. The process description on Services makes the next step clear enough that absence of price does not constitute a dead end.

## Check 4: Mobile

### Code findings

**640px (nav threshold):** Hamburger at 44px touch target. Drawer at 52px link min-height. Inline nav links hidden. Correct.

**Homepage hero at 375px:** H1 at `clamp(2rem, 9vw, 2.75rem)` — approximately 34px at 375px. Readable. Lead at 1.125rem. Spacing tightened correctly.

**Services at 640px:** Service block header at 52px column + 16px gap + 24px title. Body collapses to 1-col. Sidebar CTA pill goes full-width. Sticky aside becomes `position: static` at 768px. Correct.

**About page at 640px:** Portrait moves to `order: -1` (above text), becomes 1/1 square with 12px border-radius. Facts grid collapses to 1-col at 760px. Find-link-row reflows to 2-row layout. Correct.

**Footer at 480px:** Grid goes 1-col with `text-align: center`. Footer-bottom stacks. The historical cascade conflict (inline style block overriding site.css media query) was resolved by the CSS refactor into homepage.css. In current code, the 480px rule in site.css applies without competition.

**Fixed-width risk:** `.about-portrait` at base is 300px x 300px. At 768px it reduces to 200px; at 640px the grid becomes flex column so the 200px portrait sits in a full-width column. At 375px, 200px is safe. No overflow risk in code.

**Potential issue:** `.proof-cards` sits outside `.wrap` (max-width 1440px, margin auto). At ≤768px it collapses to 1-col with padding at `36px 28px 32px`. At 375px this is 28px horizontal padding per side: 375 - 56 = 319px usable width. Fine.

**Performance concern (mobile):** The tweaks-panel scripts on index.html (lines 339-372) load React 18.3.1 development build, ReactDOM 18.3.1 development build, and Babel 7.29.0 from unpkg.com on every homepage load. Development builds of React and ReactDOM are significantly larger than production builds. On a slow mobile connection, these three scripts represent avoidable load. This was flagged in the security audit (2026-06-10) as a should-fix; it is also a mobile performance issue.

### Named manual checks

1. DevTools 375px, homepage: verify diagnosis band text does not overflow the viewport when the `diag-br` forced break is absent on mobile.
2. DevTools 375px, Services: verify anchor bar scrolls horizontally without labels clipping outside viewport bounds.
3. Real iOS device (Safari): verify ↗&#xFE0E; (text variant selector U+FE0E) renders as text, not emoji, in WebKit.
4. Real iOS device: double-sticky on Services (nav at top:0, anchor bar at top:56px) — confirm scroll-to-anchor targets do not land behind both bars simultaneously.
5. Real device, any: scroll-to-top button visible and tappable over grain overlay on homepage. z-index 9001 vs 9000 in code should be correct; verify on a physical device.
6. Slow network simulation: measure load time of homepage with tweaks-panel scripts present. Remove or gate before treating the site as performance-ready.

## Check 5: The voice

The copy sounds like a person. Specific markers:

The diagnosis band on the homepage is the clearest voice on the site: "Activity is full. Output is steady. But the strategy underneath has thinned, or never quite got named, and the gap between what marketing and communications is producing and what the organisation actually needs is now visible from the outside." This is not template copy. A competitor cannot paste this onto their site without it sounding wrong, because it describes a specific diagnosis from a specific vantage point.

The About background is equally personal: "My instinct for this work started in museums." That is a real entry point. "Read the gap between what was there and what the person in front of it could hear, and close it without flattening either side." Specific, earned, not borrowable.

The "Work in practice" section on About is the strongest passage on the site: "Positioning that holds across a fractured organisation. Messaging that moves people who weren't going to be moved. An internal narrative that makes a restructure feel like something the team is part of rather than something happening to them." That is outcome writing from direct experience. It passes the sameness test entirely.

Services copy is procedural by necessity and appropriately tighter, but avoids filler. "The audit finds the gap between what is being produced and what the organisation actually needs." Active, direct. "Not a theoretical overview, not a general introduction to AI." Confident.

**Filler found:**

- Proof section sub-heading at 0.68 opacity: "If you'd like to see how I think before we speak." This reads like a placeholder rather than a sentence Jasmin would say aloud. It is not wrong but it is the weakest line on the page.
- Contact page intro: "A basic understanding of organizational challenges is sufficient to begin a conversation." This is more clinical than the rest of the page and also uses American spelling. Check the source; this may be a copy carry-over from an earlier draft.

**Sameness test results:**
- Diagnosis band: passes. No competitor owns this.
- About background: passes. The museums origin is individual.
- Campaign strategy description: borderline. "Fundraising push, a public awareness moment, an advocacy campaign, an internal change programme" is a list any strategic comms consultant could write. The surrounding copy is stronger.
- All other service descriptions: pass.

**Against brand intent (from CLAUDE.md: "Grounded. Rigorous. Human."):** The copy delivers grounded and rigorous across all pages. The human element comes through most on About and in the diagnosis band. Services is where rigour slightly crowds out human — this is appropriate for a money page and does not constitute a failure.

## The fractional question: summary finding

The visitor from the brief arrives asking "what does fractional mean here?" The site answers in three places:

1. Homepage "How I work" band (fourth content section, requires scrolling past hero, diagnosis, and What I Do): one sentence, clear.
2. Services tier 2 "Fractional" shape block (requires reading past five service definitions, approximately 1,800 words in): a full paragraph, the best answer on the site.
3. Services FAQ schema: exists in structured data for search engines but is not rendered on the page for human readers.

The definition is accurate, clear, and present. Its placement means a visitor who arrives specifically to understand the fractional model — and who is not a dedicated reader — is likely to miss the best answer. The fix is not writing new copy but surfacing what exists earlier. The hero lead line "Fractional or per project" already names the format; it is a natural home for a short phrase that answers "what does fractional mean?" without requiring a scroll.

## One sentence a stranger would use to describe this site to a friend

"She's a strategic comms consultant who works with charities, and the site actually explains what she does and why it matters rather than just listing services."

That sentence is specific enough to be useful. It does not yet include "fractional" or give the friend a concrete reason to click — which means the fractional positioning is not landing in the first pass, and the most important differentiator in Jasmin's offer is not yet the thing a stranger walks away saying.
