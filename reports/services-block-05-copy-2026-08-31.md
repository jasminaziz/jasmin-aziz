## 1. `.svc-main-copy`

Target 95–115 words, matching siblings 01–04's descriptive density (audit, brand, campaign, content all run in
that band). Job: earn the click to `/ai`, not duplicate it. Both candidates paraphrase the page's own governance
and training language rather than quoting it, keep the resourcing scope (sensitive data, a board, no in-house
counsel or technical team) out of this string and into `.svc-right-for` instead, where the siblings put audience
scope, and avoid enumerating the AI page's four room types by name or number (the brief bans counts outright, and
listing all four would be the block replicating the page rather than pointing to it).

### Candidate 1 — Recommended (111 words)

> AI outgrew its place as a single line on this list, so it moved to a page of its own. Governance starts with
> finding where AI is already in use, including the tools nobody signed off, and builds towards a policy short
> enough for a non-specialist to run, with data handling matched to how sensitive each tool is, and a plan for
> reputational harm agreed before anyone needs it. Training runs the other way: it starts from a diagnostic of
> your team's real work, not a generic outline, and builds the room to match, whether that's a working session or
> one-to-one time with a chief executive. Two different problems, solved differently.

**Against the spec:** Opens by naming the fact, not selling. One colon only, no rule-of-three, no em dash, active
verbs throughout ("starts", "builds", "runs"). "Not a generic outline" is the earned "not X" boundary the spec
explicitly allows (drawing a boundary around the offer, not a rhetorical flourish with nothing behind it), and it
mirrors the AI page's own "without touching a tutorial" / "not just drafted" moves without quoting either. Closing
line "Two different problems, solved differently" is a compressed landing beat (spec's short-declarative rule,
"Short declaratives carry weight"), not a manufactured punchline, since it does real work: it tells the reader the
block covers two jobs, which is the whole reason it's being expanded.
**Trade-off against Candidate 2:** drops the currently live opening pair for a tighter single sentence with an
active verb ("outgrew") in subject position. Slightly more economical, slightly less continuity with what's shipped.

### Candidate 2 — Alternative, keeps the live opening (114 words)

> AI has grown beyond a line on this list. Governance for the organisations accountable for it, and training for
> the teams using it, now have a page of their own. Governance starts with finding where AI is already in use,
> including the tools nobody signed off, then works towards a policy short enough for a non-specialist to run,
> data handling matched to how sensitive each tool is, and a plan for reputational harm agreed before anyone needs
> it. Training starts from a diagnostic of what your team actually does, not a generic outline, and the session
> gets built from what that finds. Either way, you're the one who has to answer for it.

**Against the spec:** Same governance/training paraphrase as Candidate 1, same "not a generic outline" boundary.
Keeps "actually" once, in "what your team actually does" — this is the one place I'd flag it against humanizer's
Rule 11 (trim reflexive intensifiers), but it survives the spec's own test because it's contrastive (real work
versus an assumed generic version, echoing the AI page's "your team's real tasks"), not an adjective inflator. The
closing line "you're the one who has to answer for it" trades the tidier landing of Candidate 1 for something
closer to the site's habit of ending on accountability rather than on a formal beat.
**Trade-off against Candidate 1:** preserves continuity with the two sentences already live and signed off in an
earlier session, at the cost of a passive-feeling opening subject ("Governance for the organisations accountable
for it... now have a page of their own") that Candidate 1's active phrasing avoids.

## 2. `.svc-right-for`

One sentence, opening "Organisations that...", matching the sibling pattern. Carries the resourcing scope from the
AI page: sensitive data, answers to a board, no in-house counsel or technical team. Never sector-scoped.

### Option A — Recommended (23 words)

> Organisations that hold sensitive data and answer to a board, with no in-house counsel or technical team to
> hand the AI question to.

**Against the spec:** Plain audience statement, same register as the four siblings' Right For lines (e.g. block
03: "Organisations with a significant campaign moment ahead and a team ready to deliver it, who need the strategic
architecture in place first"). Does the one job a Right For line does: names who this is for, nothing more.

### Option B — Alternative, diagnostic opening (30 words)

> Organisations that already have AI in use, whether the board has agreed to it or not, holding sensitive data
> with no in-house counsel or technical team to make the call.

**Against the spec:** Leans on the spec's "open by naming a pattern, not by selling" mechanic and echoes the AI
page's own opening hook ("Your organisation is already using AI, whether or not the board has decided to") without
quoting it. More distinctive, more voice-forward.
**Trade-off:** the siblings' Right For lines are plain audience descriptions, not diagnostic hooks; that job
normally belongs to the main copy or the hero. Option B does slightly more work than a Right For line is built to
do elsewhere on this page. Choose it only if you want block 05 to carry a bit of the AI page's own energy this far
up the funnel.
